# What this is

A living playbook for agentic coding. It **extracts the real solutions and approaches used and being built in Tagwell as we work**—not compiled from other guides, and not aspirational filler.

## Purpose and source

- **Purpose:** capture what actually works in real projects and make it reusable when starting or refactoring a project.
- **Source of truth:** Tagwell is the living source and stays read-only. Durable, tested lessons are distilled here as they appear, so the guide follows the practice—not the other way around.
- **Audience:** developers setting up or refactoring an agentic-coding workflow; agents that read the guide as reference; projects that copy its templates or install its patterns.

## What it is not

- Not a generic best-practices list. If a section only restates common advice, it does not belong here.
- Not copy-paste code or configuration. Wrapper specs and examples are shapes to implement in your own stack.
- Not organization-wide compliance governance, and not (yet) security/PII hardening.

## How it stays real

Every claim is either a tested lesson or is labelled with its limits (dated estimates, prices, and results). A practice earns its place by working, not by sounding reasonable.

## How to use it

- **Read** — start at the [playbook](playbook.md).
- **Reference** — point a target project's session at this guide, read-only, so it can assess and refactor in place.
- **Copy** — take the [reusable templates](templates.md) into a project.
- **Install** — turn repeating contracts into skills, agents, commands, wrappers, and checks.

Details and rules for all four are in [Bring the playbook into a project](bring-playbook-to-project.md).

## How it is maintained

See the [working agreement](working-agreement.md). In short: extract, generalize, keep one canonical copy, delete what stops earning its place, and record meaningful changes in the change log on the [playbook page](playbook.md).
